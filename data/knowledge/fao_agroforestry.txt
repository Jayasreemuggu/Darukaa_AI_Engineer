TITLE: Agroforestry and Biodiversity
SOURCE: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)
SOURCE_URL: https://www.fao.org/americas/priorities/agricultura-sostenible/agrofesteria/en

FAO describes agroforestry as the integration of trees with crops and/or livestock. Agroforestry can support biodiversity and ecosystem services including soil fertility and water regulation.

FAO identifies interactions among trees, crops and livestock that can improve the use of light, water and nutrients. Agroforestry can contribute to soil restoration, biodiversity, water regulation and landscape resilience.

Benefits accumulate over time as trees grow, soils recover and ecological interactions develop. Outcomes depend on local conditions and management.

KEY VARIABLES:
- Land use
- Vegetation diversity
- Soil organic carbon
- Water regulation
- Biodiversity
- Climate resilience
- Landscape connectivity

RELEVANCE:
Agroforestry can be considered when the objective is to increase vegetation structural diversity while addressing soil and water-related constraints. The appropriate design must depend on local climate, soil, species and landscape context.
