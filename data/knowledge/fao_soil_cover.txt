TITLE: Soil Organic Cover and Conservation Agriculture
SOURCE: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)
SOURCE_URL: https://www.fao.org/conservation-agriculture/in-practice/soil-organic-cover/en/

Cover crops and crop residues help maintain soil cover. FAO describes cover crops as beneficial for protecting soil, improving soil structure, recycling nutrients and supporting biodiversity in agro-ecosystems.

Cover crops can be particularly useful in semi-arid or degraded areas. They can utilize residual soil moisture, provide organic matter and improve water infiltration. Mulch from plant residues can reduce evaporation of soil moisture and improve infiltration.

FAO also describes crop diversification as part of conservation agriculture. Diversification can help break monoculture systems and support biological processes above and below the soil surface.

KEY VARIABLES:
- Soil organic matter
- Soil moisture
- Soil structure
- Water infiltration
- Crop diversity
- Biological activity
- Soil biodiversity

RELEVANCE:
For a low-rainfall monoculture system with poor soil condition, soil cover and crop diversification are potentially relevant interventions. Site-specific crop selection and water availability must still be considered.
