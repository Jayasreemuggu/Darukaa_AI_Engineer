TITLE: Agricultural Biodiversity
SOURCE: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)
SOURCE_URL: https://www.fao.org/agriculture/crops/thematic-sitemap/theme/compendium/tools-guidelines/what-is-agricultural-biodiversity/en/

FAO describes agricultural biodiversity as important for ecosystem functions including soil fertility, water regulation, nutrient cycling and pollination.

Crop rotations, crop mixtures, permanent soil cover and agroforestry are examples of practices that can increase planned biodiversity within agricultural systems.

Soil organisms contribute to decomposition, nutrient cycling and soil structure. Crop-associated biodiversity can contribute to pest regulation, pollination, water quality, soil moisture retention and erosion control.

KEY VARIABLES:
- Crop diversity
- Soil biodiversity
- Soil fertility
- Soil moisture
- Pollination
- Pest regulation
- Nutrient cycling

RELEVANCE:
Monoculture can be evaluated as one component of a broader biodiversity problem. Crop diversification should be considered together with soil, water, habitat and landscape conditions rather than as an isolated intervention.
